Questions churches ask

Straight answers.

Does Modulus Studio write sermons?

No. Studio never writes, drafts, or generates a sermon, and it is not designed to. The pastor does the sacred work of study, prayer, and preaching. Studio starts after the message is preached and only repurposes it into other formats like devotionals, articles, social posts, and a clean transcript. It works downstream of the pulpit, never upstream of it.

How does it keep from putting words in the preacher's mouth?

Studio works only from the actual sermon you provide as audio, video, or a transcript. The sermon is the only source, and every draft is checked against it, so the tool does not invent quotes the pastor never said or add claims from outside the message. If a line is not in the sermon, it does not make it into the draft. A person still reviews each piece before it is published.

What is the Theological Judge and how does it work?

The Theological Judge is a doctrine-aware check you configure for your church. You set your frame once, such as Reformed, Baptist, Pentecostal, a specific confession, or your own statement of faith, and Studio flags anything in a draft that drifts from that teaching. It does not overrule the pastor or decide what is true. It surfaces possible drift so a person can make the call. This keeps content faithful both to what was preached and to your church's distinctives.

What can one sermon become?

From a single message, Studio produces written content including devotionals, blog articles, short question-and-answer pieces, key quotes, social media posts, and a clean, accurate transcript for your website or archive. The common pattern is one Sunday sermon becoming a month of content. Studio focuses on the words, which is the part AI most often gets wrong, rather than producing video clips or image graphics.

Is our congregation's private data safe?

Studio only ever works with the sermon you already preached in public. It does not ask for and does not need your membership list, giving records, prayer requests, or counseling notes. It runs as a Windows desktop app, and the only input is the public-facing sermon. Sensitive congregation data is never part of the workflow.
